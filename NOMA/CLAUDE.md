## The Seven Operating Rules

**R1 — Ground truth is the disk.**
Before starting or resuming any phase, list the output directories and check which deliverables actually exist. Never trust a status marker, a summary, a memory, or your own earlier claim. A phase is complete when its file is on disk and passes its exit check, not when a table says done.

**R2 — Target Journal Guidelines outrank everything.**
The IEEE Communications Letters official *Information for Authors* beats your training data, beats general academic conventions, and beats author preferences. Where the guide is silent, recent papers published in IEEE Communications Letters serve as the secondary authority. Where both are silent, state the ambiguity in writing rather than defaulting quietly.

**R3 — Citation provenance and DOI verification.**
Every citation must resolve to a physical source PDF/file present in `Literatures/` or a verified DOI/BibTeX record in `literatures_inventory.json` / `references.bib`. No citation is ever invented, half-remembered, or inferred from a titles-only search. Every reference must meet IEEE style requirements (numbered, DOI mandatory where available).

**R4 — Integrity and no fabricated/cherry-picked evidence.**
Nothing is simulated, interpolated, imputed, or omitted without explicit methodological justification. Outliers, negative results, or non-significant findings must be reported if generated by the protocol. All SimPy simulation parameters, random seeds, and Python package versions must be documented in `docs/Data_Code_Manifest.md`.

**R5 — Sequential phases and results-first workflow.**
Figures, tables, and simulation results must be locked before the Discussion or Conclusion is drafted. No phase begins until the previous phase's deliverables exist and its exit check passes, except where the critical path allows parallel tasks (see execution plan).

**R6 — Placeholders are declared, never invented.**
ORCID iDs, grant numbers, conflict of interest disclosures, ethics approval IDs, corresponding author details: if unknown, write `[NEEDS_X: description]` and list the item in the Open Items table. Never fill a gap with plausible placeholder text.

**R7 — The golden thread of scientific novelty.**
Research Gap → Target Question/Hypothesis → Method → Key Empirical Finding → Implication/Claim → Explicit Limitation. Every section must trace directly to this thread. A section that introduces unanchored claims or unaddressed questions is a defect and must be flagged immediately.
